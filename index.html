Can you pick the right task order before you train?

When a neural network learns tasks one after another, it forgets the earlier ones, and how much it forgets depends on the order. Most research asks how to stop the forgetting. This thesis asks a practitioner's question instead: can a cheap signal tell you a good order before you spend the compute, without training all n! of them?

00 · THE PROBLEM

Order matters, but brute force doesn't scale

Fraud detectors, medical-imaging pipelines and recommenders all learn from data that arrives over time. If you have any say in the sequence, you'd like the order that forgets least. The catch is that there are n! orders, and judging each one means training on all of it.

120
orderings, each a full training run

This thesis works at n = 5 on purpose. That is small enough to train every ordering and use the results as exact ground truth when testing cheap predictors. The predictors are only worth having at n ≳ 10, where the full search is out of reach.

01 · EXPERIMENT 1

Is total forgetting conserved? No. It's mostly about who goes last.

Split-MNIST, divided into five two-digit tasks, trained in sequence by a small MLP with one shared output head. All 120 orderings were trained over two seeds. Mean forgetting ranges from 0.271 to 0.665 depending only on order, so order is a real lever. But the identity of the last task alone explains R² ≈ 0.96 of that variation.

Why? Where in the network the damage happens

Three candidate mechanisms: gradient scale (the last task pushes hardest), capacity reallocation (features get overwritten), and readout disruption (the features survive but the classifier can't read them). I froze the backbone at the end of the sequence and trained a fresh linear probe on it.

97.9%

median share of the single-head → per-task-head accuracy gap that the frozen backbone recovers (n = 76). The information is still there. The classifier just can't read it out.

The backbone does drift (CKA falls from 0.72 to about 0.47). It reorganises without losing class information, and the frozen head stays calibrated to a representation that has moved on. Readout disruption and drift turn out to be two views of the same process.

CKA similarity vs. stages before the end. Single-batch and 5-batch estimates agree (ρ ≈ −0.43, p = 0.0001).

02 · EXPERIMENT 2

Reproducing the strongest prior method, and finding a hidden degree of freedom

Bell & Lawrence (2022) order tasks along a maximum-curvature path built from an asymmetric matrix of pairwise task distances. Turning that prose definition into a general matrix-and-solver pipeline forces a choice: which axis of the matrix the solver reads as "source". The original never had to make that choice explicit. In this reproduction, statistical significance depends on it.

Reproduction at the original sample sizes

Δ = forgetting(max-path) − forgetting(min-path), 95% CI. Negative means the effect points the way the paper predicts.

The direction holds wherever the effect is non-negligible. Significance does not: under the paper's orientation the effect reaches significance at the boundary on one dataset, is marginal on a second, and fails on the other two.

Flip the seed, flip the orientation that "works"

Gratings dataset, same pipeline and same N = 17. Only the base seed changes. Corrected p-values for each way of reading the matrix:

Choosing whichever orientation passes, after seeing the results, is a hidden experimenter degree of freedom. It inflates the family-wise error rate by up to 3×, and a standard Bonferroni correction applied within one orientation doesn't catch it. This is a reproducibility risk for any ordering method built on an asymmetric matrix.

I audited the code paths line by line and checked them numerically (a 15–32× separation on well-conditioned pairs). The forward reading is the paper-faithful one.

03 · EXPERIMENT 3

A cheap, pre-training signal built from machinery you already run

EWC-style regularisation already computes a Fisher importance score for every weight. I repurposed it as a conflict detector: how strongly does a candidate next task's gradient push on the weights the current task cares about? The score is computed before the candidate is trained, from a single converged model.

The victim-isolated design

Fix a victim VTrain one probe on V to convergence. Every score is computed from this single shared point in weight space.
Score two partnersEstimate how much P₁ and P₂ would each disrupt V. No training on either partner.
PredictFlag the partner that is safer to train next.
CheckTrain V→P₁ and V→P₂ for real and compare forgetting on V. V's own fragility cancels out.

Getting here took fixing two confounds that each made the signal look broken. (1) Comparing Fisher matrices from independently initialised networks is meaningless, because their hidden units sit in arbitrary bases (the problem CKA exists to solve). (2) With only two tasks, "which order is safer?" collapses into "which task is more fragile?".

Design, not signal quality, decided detectability

How much should you believe 65%?

The 60 triples come from only 6 victims and 30 distinct scores, so I re-tested with resampling schemes that respect that structure:

  • Binomial (assumes independence)p = 0.0137
  • Within-victim permutationp = 0.046
  • Exact task relabelling (720)p = 0.069
  • Victim-level cluster bootstrap95% CI [0.517, 0.767]
Verdict: marginally above chance, directional but not calibrated, and its reliability depends on each task's role (the hit rate ranges from 90% to 40% by victim). It's the thesis's first statistically supported evidence that ordering outcomes can be predicted before training.

But it doesn't compose into a full ranking (yet)

I scored all 120 orderings with three ways of aggregating the pairwise scores and checked each method's top pick against the exact ground truth. This is the true rank of each pick out of 120, where lower is better (Roster A).

Adding a recency term helps, but only when that term is an oracle computed from the answers. Five honest pre-training substitutes recover none of its advantage. Experiment 1 explains why: the effect is mostly positional, and a signal built only from adjacent pairs can't see position.

05 · WHAT IT ADDS UP TO

Takeaways and what's next

Established

Order is a real lever, and its mechanism is localised

Forgetting is not conserved. With a shared head, the effect is dominated by recency and lives in the classifier readout, not in lost features.

Methodological

Asymmetric-matrix methods hide a choice

The prior method's direction reproduces, but its significance depends on an orientation convention that falls outside standard multiple-comparison control.

Constructive

Pairwise prediction works, narrowly

A Fisher-based score beats chance before training and costs nothing extra for teams already running EWC. Composing it into a full ranking is the open problem.

Honest limits and the next experiments

  • Scale: all experiments use five or six small tasks so that ground truth is exact. Whether the signal pays off at n ≳ 10 is the deciding question.
  • Benchmark ceiling: on Split-CIFAR-10, retention drops to chance across a 30-config capacity sweep (8× width, MLP and CNN). That floor is structural, so testing recency "off the ceiling" needs fewer tasks or a retention mechanism.
  • Mitigation: does any ordering benefit survive once EWC or replay is switched on? This needs a factorial study.
  • Bake-off: Fisher conflict vs. curvature vs. CKA overlap, with a combined predictor, run in a regime where recency doesn't saturate the target.
